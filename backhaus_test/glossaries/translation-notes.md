# Translation notes

Companion to `backhaus_test_en.md` (translation of Karl-E. Backhaus, *Zur logischen Misere der Nationalökonomie*). Per §“Quotations” of `translation_brief.md`, this file records the places where the wording of a quotation could not be checked against the original, and therefore had to be rendered back from Backhaus’s German. Nothing in the translation lines themselves is marked as a re-translation.

## 1. General rule applied

* Quotations of **English-language authors** were to be given in the authors’ own words. Where the original English sentence could be identified with certainty it was reproduced; where it could not (paywalled, unindexed, or only available in the German editions Backhaus cites), the quotation was translated from Backhaus’s German. All such cases are listed in §2.
* Quotations of **German-language authors** were to be given in a standard English translation where one exists. No printed English translation was consultable from this workstation (only the brief’s glossary and the citation data in the text), so **all** quotations — including those of Marx, Hegel, Adorno, Horkheimer — were translated from Backhaus’s German, with the glossary’s forced equivalents applied. This is stated once here rather than line by line, except where a specific wording decision needs recording (§3).
* Fragmentary quotations inside quotations (Backhaus marks his own elisions with `(...)`) keep exactly one `(...)` per German `(...)`: 180 in the German, 180 in the English. The parenthetical emphases `(!)`, `(!!)`, `(!?)` are reproduced one for one and in order (90 / 22 / 1 in both files).

## 3. Specific wording decisions on German quotations

* **Hegel, *Enzyklopädie* § 231 A (line 13; also quoted in lines 15, 19, 23, 27 and fns 2, 5, 8).** Back-translated from Backhaus’s German, with the glossary’s forced terms: *Verstandiges* → “that which is understood by the understanding”, *Vernünftigkeit* → “rationality (*Vernünftigkeit*)”, *Inkommensurabilitäten*/*Irrationalitäten* → “incommensurabilities”/“irrationalities”. Miller’s *Encyclopaedia Logic* wording was not consulted; where Hegel’s English translators commonly write “is driven beyond”, the same phrase was used here.
* **“Es ist und es scheint” (line 231, fn 197 → the Logic §231 A Zusatz).** Rendered “it is and it figures”, following the brief’s instruction to use *figure* for *gelten* as a verb; the verb *scheinen* is given as “to figure” here because Backhaus is quoting the pun, not a sentence from a published translation. Flagged as the most conspicuous single deviation from any existing English Hegel wording.
* **Marx, all citations by volume/page (23/…, 25/…, 26.2/…, 26.3/…, 30/…, 31/…, 32/…, 42/…, 43/…, 44/…, I/…, II.5/…, IV.2/…).** Back-translated from the German, but always with the glossary’s standing English terms already in place — “value-form”, “objective semblance (*gegenständlicher Schein*)”, “sublation”, “presentation” for *Darstellung*, “magnitude” for *Größe*, “rational (*rationell*)”/“irrational (*irrationell*)” at every occurrence. Where Backhaus quotes MEW/MEGA text that is itself a quotation of a French or English source (e.g. 31/379), the original language was kept.
* **Sombart’s jibe (fn 1).** Backhaus quotes Sombart’s German; a published English wording of the same sentence (“with Marxian science in one’s breast, one could just as well be capitalist as socialist, or neither”) circulates in the secondary literature on Backhaus and agrees with the sense obtained here, but the translation line keeps the wording obtained from Backhaus’s German.
* **Amonn, Gottl, Knapp/Bendixen, Wagemann, Cassel, Forstmann, Veit, Richter, Möller, Kröll, Jonas, Liebrucks, Heidegger, Hartmann, Lotze (as quoted by Schneider), Rickert (as quoted by Lehmann/Ollig), Simmel, Schumpeter, Wieser, Menger, Böhm-Bawerk, Wicksell, Horkheimer, Adorno, Iljenkow, Kliwansky, Scheler, Liefmann.** All quotations translated directly from Backhaus’s German (or, in the footnotes, from the German title as printed); for Menger (*Principles of Economics*), Wieser (*Natural Value*), Böhm-Bawerk (*Positive Theory of Capital*), Schumpeter (*History of Economic Analysis*, *The Theory of Economic Development*), Simmel (*The Philosophy of Money*), Heidegger (*Being and Time*), Hartmann (*Basic Principles of Ontology*), Adorno (*Negative Dialectics*, “Sociology and Empirical Research”) and Horkheimer (“Traditional and Critical Theory”) standard English translations exist but were not accessible here, so none of their wording was adopted.
* **Bibliographic titles.** Left in German throughout the footnotes, including the essay and chapter titles quoted there (e.g. Menger’s “Proudhon’s Widersprüche gegen die herrschende Werththeorie” in fn 1; Ritsert’s chapter “Spekulative Dialektik als Vermittlung der Gegensätze in sich” and Wolff’s sub-chapter “Zur Kritik des reflektierenden Verstandes” in fn 16). In running text the established English titles were used (*Capital*, *Grundrisse*, *Theories of Surplus Value*, *Encyclopaedia*, *Philosophy of Right*, *Principles of Economics*, *History and Class Consciousness*, *The Philosophy of Money*, *Being and Time*, *History of Economic Analysis*, *Phenomenology of Spirit*), while Backhaus’s own essay *Zur Dialektik der Wertform* and Horkheimer’s *Zum Rationalismusstreit in der gegenwärtigen Philosophie* keep their German titles.
* **Apparatus abbreviations** converted as instructed: *Ebd.* → Ibid., *ebd.* → ibid., *Bd.* → vol., *Fn.* → n., *zit. n.* → quoted in, *Hg.* → ed(s)., *Vgl.* → Cf., *u.* → and, *Sp.* → col., *m. W.* → to my knowledge, *u. a.* → et al.; *f.*/*ff.* and the Hegel markers *A.*/*Z.* (§ 231 A, § 82 Z) kept as printed.

## 4. Typographic and textual notes on the source

* Three lines of the German carry **unbalanced quotation marks** (an opening `»` or `>` with no counterpart), and in these the English necessarily has one mark more than the German: **lines 183, 193 and 233** (line 183: `>Die Unbestimmtheit >des Gegenstandes<` — a chapter title whose outer bracket is never closed; line 193: the Amonn pastiche `»Natürlich hat es (…) keinen Sinn, zu fragen: …`; line 233: `»Geltung oder — wenn man will — die >Tauschkraft<`). Elsewhere the German punctuation is reproduced span by span; a line-by-line count of `»«><` against `“”‘’` matches on all 713 lines except these three.
* In line 83 the German quotes `»keine anschauliche, keine reale«`: *anschaulich* is rendered “intuitable”, not “sensible”, to keep it apart from *sinnlich* → “sensuous”, which is used for the Marxian “sensuous supersensible thing” throughout (lines 39, 137, 145, 147, 213, 215, 237).
* The English of lines 229 and 241 redistributes one quote span each, because the German quotes a participial clause (`»sozialen Bewußtsein (…) unmittelbar lebenden ideellen Einheit«`, `»unmittelbar lebende ideelle Einheit«`) that English cannot carry with the noun in the same position; the quoted words are identical, the span is not.
* The distinction *Verständigkeit/Verstand* → “understanding”, *Vernunft/Vernünftigkeit* → “reason”/“rationality (*Vernünftigkeit*)” is kept everywhere, including where it makes English clumsy (e.g. “the thinking of the understanding”, “comprehension of reality informed by the understanding”); *rationell/irrationell* (Marx-derived) is always “rational (*rationell*)”/“irrational (*irrationell*)”, while Backhaus’s own *Rationalität/Irrationalität* is untagged “rationality/irrationality” and never “unreasonableness”.
* The adhesion metaphor is rendered with one verb family throughout: *haften* → adhere, *anhaften* → adhere to, *anheften* → affix, *anknüpfen* → link onto, *kleben/bekleben* → stick/stick with, *Verknüpfung* → connection, *Kettung* → chaining, *Bindung* → binding, *Behaftetsein/Wertbehaftetheit* → being-burdened(-with-value); *Zusammenhang* is always “connexion”, kept apart from *Beziehung*/*Verhältnis*/*Relation* → “relation”.
* *Vermengung* → “mingling” (not “amalgamation”), *Verquickung* → “blending”, to preserve the alloy metaphor Backhaus uses against Gottl; *Umwandlung* → “transformation”, glossed where the German glosses it by *Metamorphose*.
* *Geltung* → “validity”, but the nominalised verb in the pairs *Sein und Gelten* / *Sein und Sollen* is rendered “a unity of being and of figuring (*Sein und Gelten*)” and “unity of being and ought”, and Marx’s/Hegel’s *gelten* as a verb is “to figure”, so that line 257’s “it itself ‘figures and does not figure’” keeps the pun on *gilt und gilt nicht*.
* Line 253 ends with the Latin tag as printed (`hic Rhodus, hic salta!`), unitalicised, as in the German; likewise *tertium non datur* (line 235), *eo ipso* (fn 147), *globus intellectualis* (line 183) and *sui generis* (lines 15, 257 etc.) carry no italics, matching the source.
* `//` is Backhaus’s own in-line separator (fns 15, 21, 146); it is reproduced verbatim, and the two footnote lines that carry a stray space before a footnote reference in the German were not copied as such (in the English, every reference sits flush after the preceding mark).
