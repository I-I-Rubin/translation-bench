# Glossary and notes for the new Mill translation

This is a supplement to `glossary.md`, not a replacement for it. The English text in `../mill_haiku_en.md` is translated anew from the supplied German, not revised from an existing English translation. Its line divisions follow `../mill_haiku_de.md`; the German source is unchanged. Line numbers below refer to both aligned files.

## Working glossary

The project glossary governs the translation. The following records the principal choices actually used here, including the prompt's specifications for the 1844 vocabulary.

## Contextual and textual decisions

### Opening: *Kompensation* (line 7)

“Jener” points backward to the discussion summarized in the source's editorial note 1: the determination of money value by metal value and the regulation of metal value by the costs of production. Following the contextual investigation undertaken before this fresh translation, “equalization” names the movement bringing the two values into agreement. The expanded English “the value of money and the value of the metal” makes explicit this reading of the compressed *des Geldes und Metallwerts*. It is a contextual interpretation, not a claim that the German literally repeats *Wert* twice. It must not be confused with *gleichsetzen*, “set equal.”

The remainder of the paragraph opposes necessity and contingency and distinguishes the actual movement from a fixed abstract moment. “Equalization” therefore names a process, not an assertion that equality is permanently given.

### Properties of the species-activity (line 9)

In “Alle Eigenschaften, welche **dieser** in der Produktion dieser Tätigkeit zukommen,” *dieser* is read as a feminine dative referring to *Gattungstätigkeit*. Accordingly: “All the properties accruing to **this species-activity** …” Assigning the properties directly to *der Mensch* would require a masculine dative (*diesem*). The repeated “production of this activity” is retained rather than silently rewritten.

### Credit and payment (line 21)

“Sich … den Kredit nicht zahlen lässt” is translated “does not charge for the credit.” It is not rendered as foregoing repayment of the principal: the following paragraph explicitly treats repayment together with customary interest as the creditor's expectation.

### Doubtful wording in the supplied German

These are cautions about the supplied text, not verified emendations against a manuscript or critical edition:

- **Line 23:** “in a twofold manner” is retained although four numbered items follow. The translation does not silently renumber or rewrite the claim.
- **Line 27:** the supplied word is *Vorstellung*, translated “representation” in accordance with the glossary. The context of dissimulation and hypocrisy might invite the conjecture *Verstellung*, but that is not the word supplied and has not been substituted. The unusual *demoralische Urteil* is interpreted as a morally condemnatory judgment; this is not a claim that *demoralisch* is a regular technical category.
- **Line 31:** the concluding pronoun in “was er wirklich ist” has an uncertain antecedent in the supplied sentence. The English does not supply a new explanatory subject.
- **Line 79 and note 9:** *Geist* is translated “spirit” at both places. The source's note reporting the alternative reading *Genuss* (“enjoyment”) is retained rather than silently adopted.
- **Line 127:** the supplied text says “**nie wieder** eine Gnade antue.” The English therefore retains “**never again** bestow a favor,” despite the difficulty this creates in the argument. It does not introduce a conjectural *mir wieder* or *dir wieder* in place of *nie wieder*. Establishing a different reading requires checking a textual witness.
- **Line 135:** *gesch[ieht]* is rendered as the completed English word “happens”; the German retains the indication of a partial-word editorial completion. The inserted *[für]* of line 23 is absorbed into English syntax rather than assigned an artificial word-for-word bracket equivalent.
- **Line 141:** the supplied *weil* is rendered “because,” not silently changed to “as” or treated as a conjectural *wie*.
- **Heading and note 1:** “Pariser” in the heading and “Parisot” in the note are both retained as supplied.
- **Notes 9 and 11:** the supplied `MEGA[^2]` markup and the trailing `-*3` are retained. They are source-formatting oddities, not new cross-references introduced by the translator.
- **Note 16:** *Jahresprodukt* is translated “annual product,” although the French phrase in the text includes *brut*. The editorial note has not silently been expanded to “gross annual product.”

French expressions in the excerpts and bibliographic titles remain in French where the German leaves them in French. Existing source footnotes are translated on their corresponding lines. The independent translator's commentary stays in this file so that it does not disturb the bilingual alignment.
