I am hoping for a close translation of mill_*_de.md into English file mill_*_en.md  They are, as you see to be zipped for bilingual view, and edited side by side, so it is essential that the files correspond exactly line by line.  

The translation should be as careful as possible to respect the glossary.md in the glossary directory.   For the general esprit see also Fineschi glossario, but here especially 'objectual' must absolutely be rejected as that form is completely consumed in analytic philosophy; it must all be words around 'objective' as root.  I menton Fineschi because one is looking for a translation that respects philosophical content, and the received German->English for German philosophy of the period.   

Here are additional points that might be taken into account.  The glossary is for 1867 but this work in 1844. Some new terms specific to 1844, using the established English for these manuscripts might be these:

Entfremdung → estrangement; Entäußerung → alienation. Veräußerung never appears, so “alienation” has only one source here. Where Marx plays on the äußer- root, I added the German: “a thing external to me. I thus alienate (entäußere)…”, and “manifestation of life (Lebensäußerung)” against “alienation of life (Lebensentäußrung)”.
Gattungstätigkeit / Gattungsakt / Gattungsleben → species-activity / species-act / species-life.
Gemeinwesen → “communal being (*Gemeinwesen*)”. The usual English is “community”, but that loses the Wesen, and Marx’s sentence depends on it: “the human essence is the true communal being of human beings…”.
Wesen → “essence”, or “being” where it names an entity (“a sociable being”). Its compounds Geldwesen, Kreditwesen and Bankwesen are “money-essence”, “credit system” and “banking system”, with the German added.
Herr / Knecht / Knechtschaft → lord / bondsman / bondage, as in English translations of Hegel.
Erwerbsarbeit → “labor-for-gain”.
Not / Bedürfnis → want / need.
Macht / Kraft → power / force.
gesellig → “sociable”, kept apart from gesellschaftlich = social.
